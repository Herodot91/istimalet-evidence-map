# Trei surse noi — ce s-a schimbat în aplicație
*New sources — what changed in the app*

## 1. Öz, V. (2021)
‘Osmanlı Devleti’nde istimâlet siyâseti ve istimâletnâmeler’,
*Edebali İslamiyat Dergisi*, 5(2), pp. 1–18. — text accessible, read in full.

**İstimâletnâme is a document genre**, not just a practice: written in the
*nâme-i hümâyun* style, in five parts — *davet, elkâb, duâ, nâkil, tenbîh* —
addressed to administrators, commanders and local notables, and collected in
Feridun Bey’s *Münşeâtü’s-selâtîn*.

→ Added to the Effect panel as the register that would give **explicit**
attestation rather than mechanism correspondence. If an istimâletnâme exists
for Kili or Akkerman, it settles the lexical question the study separates out.

## 2. Savaş, S. ‘İstimâlet: bir Osmanlı yönetim klasiği’
in *Ötekilerin Peşinde: Ahmet Yaşar Ocak’a Armağan*. — body text read through
the Academia preview; **year not confirmed**, page range not confirmed.

Gives the canonical component list: security of life and property, freedom of
religion, release from old feudal bonds, protection of the Orthodox church and
monasteries, tax exemption, support for religious vakıfs, and absorption of the
pre-Ottoman military class with privileges intact.

Argues istimâlet is a **classical administrative approach used across the whole
of Ottoman history** — Anatolia, against the Safavids, the Arab world, the
Balkans — not a Balkan conquest tactic. That supports applying it to 1484.

**Correction it forced:** Savaş cites an *ahkâm defteri of 906/1501*, from
Bayezid II’s reign, published by Şahin & Emecen (1994). The app’s coverage
diagram said no register existed for legal continuity; the eyalet ahkâm series
does start in 1742, but this is a different, earlier, published register — and
it falls inside the window, in the right reign. The Effect panel was wrong and
is corrected.

## 3. Ünal, M.A. ‘İstimalet siyaseti çerçevesinde Osmanlı İmparatorluğu’nun
Kürd politikası’, in *Tarihte Türkler ve Kürtler Sempozyumu, Ankara,
9–10 Ocak 2014*, ed. O. Kılıç. Ankara: Türk Tarih Kurumu. — body text read;
**publication year of the proceedings not confirmed**.

Ottoman rule was **not binary**. Between the classical sancak and the tributary
principality sat the **privileged sancak** — *yurtluk-ocaklık* and *hükümet* —
where local dynasties kept their land and hereditary prerogatives inside the
empire. When governors tried to abolish those privileges, the Divan refused.

→ Added as a third instrument in the political panel. It sharpens the argument:
Kili and Akkerman received the **most direct** form available, while a gentler
in-empire option existed and was in use elsewhere. That is a choice, not an
automatic consequence of conquest.
